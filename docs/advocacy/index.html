Where this began

This work goes back to university, where I started volunteering simply because I found meaning in service — in listening, and in showing up for people who needed someone to. It began at a shelter for older adults, and later grew into teaching math to children in Lima's marginal urban neighbourhoods — work that taught me as much as I hope it gave.

From there, my engagement moved to an international stage, representing Peru in different forums. That experience pushed me to understand my own history, and my parents', more deeply — including the discrimination I sometimes faced along the way. Working within government also gave me a strong sense that this work could genuinely deliver something to people, but that delivering it well meant learning: understanding different cultures and experiences well enough to bring my own voice into the room, so that others could be included too.

The benefits of AI must be built to include everyone, not just those already positioned to capture them.

Over time I came to see how rarely indigenous and rural communities are included — not only because of how society is structured, but because the opportunity to even be in these rooms rarely reaches someone from that background in the first place. I want to help change that, even though I don't claim to fully represent those communities: I'm of mixed heritage, and I didn't live that reality as directly as my parents did. But I carry part of it with me, and it's part of why this work matters to me.

That conviction is what led me to the AI for Indigenous Communities Vertical at GYAAB, where I authored a special report on AI-driven risks and opportunities for indigenous communities, submitted to the Global AI Dialogue, and contributed the chapter "Indigenous Digital Self-Determination" to AI Multipolarity: People. Peace. Planet. It's also what takes me to rooms like the AI for Good Global Summit, the ITU Academy, and UN forums on AI governance — not as a spectator, but to make sure digital inclusion and indigenous self-determination are part of how these frameworks get built, not an afterthought once they're finished.
